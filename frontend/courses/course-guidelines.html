Instructor Guide

Create Professional-Looking Courses

Follow these guidelines to create courses that are clear, organized, attractive, and easy for students to learn from.

Recommended Workflow

Plan → Create Course → Add Lessons → Add Chapters → Add Content → Add Quizzes → Preview
1

Choose a Clear Course Title

Keep the title short, specific, and easy to understand. A learner should immediately know what the course is about.

Example: Introduction to JavaScript

2

Write a Strong Course Description

In one to three sentences, explain what the course covers, who it is for, and what students should expect to learn.

3

Use a Professional Thumbnail

Choose a clear, relevant, high-quality image. Avoid blurry images, screenshots, excessive text, and unrelated graphics.

4

Plan Your Lessons Before Creating Them

Arrange lessons from basic concepts to more advanced topics so that students can progress naturally through the course.

5

Use Descriptive Lesson Titles

A lesson title should clearly describe the topic. Avoid vague names such as "Lesson One" or "Topic Two."

6

Break Lessons into Focused Chapters

Each chapter should focus on one main idea. Several short, focused chapters are usually easier to understand than one very long chapter.

7

Keep Learning Content Readable

Use short paragraphs, clear explanations, examples, headings, and spacing. Avoid large walls of text.

8

Use Media Purposefully

Add images, videos, and resources only when they help students understand the topic. Media should support learning rather than simply decorate the page.

9

Create Meaningful Quizzes

Quiz questions should test concepts that were actually taught. Avoid trick questions and make sure every question has one clearly correct answer.

10

Preview the Course as a Student

Check the course thumbnail, lesson order, chapter order, content formatting, resources, quizzes, progress, and lesson unlocking before considering the course complete.

11

Keep the Course Consistent

Use consistent capitalization, terminology, writing style, spacing, and formatting throughout the entire course.

12

Complete a Final Quality Check

Remove test content and placeholders, correct spelling and grammar, verify links, and make sure no published chapter is accidentally empty.

Final Course Checklist

  • Clear course title
  • Professional description
  • Relevant thumbnail
  • Lessons arranged correctly
  • Chapters organized logically
  • Required chapters identified
  • Content is readable and complete
  • Media and resource links work
  • Quiz questions are correct
  • Course tested from the student view
